My game plan for landing my next role

Background

I have been in the industry for about 3 years now. My first role was an Apprenticeship at Microsoft through their LEAP program. My second role was at a company called Clover and then my third and current one is at JPMorgan & Chase Co.

I have been at JPMC for 2 years now. I have growth so much in this time, and I am going to forever be grateful for the opportunity. I have been now in two teams, where I have learned so much about Java, Microservices, APIs, team collaboration. I also have been able to learn super cool tools that I really enjoy using such as Terraform, Jenkins, GitHub copilot, and the cloud. I have used AWS since I came in here, I had no experience with AWS prior and I have had the pleasure of using a wide variety of services such as: Lambdas, EC2 instances, S3 buckets, Aurora Posgres (I love this thing), SNS, SQS, ECS, and a few others.

I have certainly enjoyed my time here. However, I can't help but feel like my career isn't growing as much or as fast as it could were I somewhere else. This is because companies in fintech have to move slower compared to companies in other sectors. The fintech industry is so heavily regulated that using the latest AI is never going to be possible, having the freedom to choose a completely different tech stack for your application, even if it makes sense won't be possible. These companies have their opinionated tools and tech stacks to do things with. Even here they have internal tools, mostly wrappers around tools such as React, Spring Boot, Jenkins, etc. Where they abstract away a ton of the actual tools and make it easy to use, but doing that make the developers not learn how they work and in my opinion, in the process it hurts them.

New plan

With all of this in mind, I have made a plan for my next role. The previous two jobs I had at Microsoft and Clover I got by literally applying to close to, or more than hundrends of companies. I was basically playing the numbers game. Just applying through LinkedIn, Indeed, and directly in companies portals and I figured eventually I'd land a job. For both of those roles it took months, about 4-6 in order to get a job. This time around I've learned a few things that I'm going to apply.

Here is what I want to put into practice this time around:

  • Target and apply to a very small number of companies that I really want to work for.
  • Modify resume for each job description. (Yes, I didn't know about this/Never did it before.)
  • Make content, contribute to open source & network.
  • Level up by applying learning concepts I've learned through books such as: 'Learning how to learn' by Barbara Oakley & 'Ultralearning' by Scott H Young.

I thought about how to find companies I'd be interested in working for. I decided to come up with a few sectors and then I picked 5-10 companies from each. Some of them were:

  • Big tech
  • Space
  • Saas Companies

Once I had a few companies to target for each sector. I went to see job openings for SWE roles, I wrote down the technologies and things they were looking for in a candidate. With that data I was able to come up with a SWE profile that matches like a general SWE role for those companies.

Besides that, I am also networking and going to try to get referrals for each of those companies before applying. That way my chances of landing an interview will be greater.

End

With all of this I'm hoping to land a new role within ~2 months. Please wish me luck.

If you are looking for a role, let me know how you're planning to go about it, and I wish you the best.
